**book-19** Why does the book recommend having the model grade its own work as the main quality check?

> The book does not recommend it; it says the opposite. It states that nothing should grade its own work, because a grader whose only input is the thing being graded is a text generator with a strong prior towards approval. A model asked whether its work is correct will usually say yes either way, so the answer tells you nothing, and the book wants checks that do not depend on the model's opinion of its own output.


**book-20** Why does the book say temperature 0 makes the model fully deterministic?

> It doesn't say that. The book says temperature 0 makes only the selection rule deterministic, because at 0 the most likely candidate is taken every time. The model itself is not deterministic: identical requests at temperature 0 can still produce different text, because of floating-point arithmetic and server-side batching outside your control.


**book-21** Why does the book say a twenty-round agent loop costs about twenty times a single request?

> The book does not say that. It says a twenty-round loop over a growing transcript does not cost twenty times one request but closer to two hundred, because you send the entire conversation every time and cost is linear in the tokens sent. Chapter 4 gives an example: a twenty-turn run whose transcript ends at 40,000 tokens sends about 400,000 input tokens, since the average turn carries half the final transcript, so cost is quadratic in turn count.
